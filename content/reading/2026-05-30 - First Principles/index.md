I really loved this article on First Principles Thinking, first seen from Ray Dalio's principles, but a way to break questions or problems into the smallest possible units that are factually true, to build back up to a answers or solutions. An example would be the problem of "I can't cook" into smaller pieces, and one way to do that is to channel your inner 5-year-old and ask "Why?" until you you get to the fundamentals

The examples that stood out to me were ones that challenged the path of least resistance, and pushed back against the "easy" answer or the one that everything thinks is true but no one really knows why. A few that resonated with me:
- That job application says it requires 10 years of experience and a master's degree - why? What does someone really need to succeed in the role?
- Netflix challenging the idea that television needs to be bound to a consistent schedule to be successful and can't be on-demand. They asked "why?" and redefined an industry

Give it a try when you find yourself defaulting to an answer that nobody can defend or explain. I think you might surprise yourself with the possibilities that come from asking "why?".
