This article talks about the incorrect assumption that you can replicate a community by setting up the same environment and inputs that created it in the first place - and I agree that it's not possible. A similar community can be created in the best case scenario, but never the same. 

When people are leaving Twitter to not support Elon and his ideals, those people can find "homes" in a new place on the internet that might look a lot like Twitter, such as BlueSky or Mastodon, but each community has some intangible that's hard to replicate. Twitter's growth with the early 140 character limit shaped the content on twitter, and no other site can have that same organic growth. 

That's an example from a digital example, and there are other disrupted communities, both in the past (mySpace, Facebook, yahoo) or in the potential future (discord, reddit). The article also talks about physical communities, that moving the same people from one place to another can never replicate the intangible glue that created that community in the first place. Worth a read.
