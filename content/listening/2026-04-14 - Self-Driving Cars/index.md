This podcast (and the one before it on the origin story of driverless cars like Waymo) does an incredible job at making me consider my point-of-view on self-driving cars. I've been to San Francisco, ridden in multiple Waymos, and enjoyed my experience quite a lot, but haven't really considered the impacts of driverless cars on the community or economy of a city. They're being [tested in Detroit](https://waymo.com/blog/shorts/newcity_detroit/) and as a consumer, I think I want it, but as a community member, I am starting to recognize the impacts to the displaced drivers and workers who are forced to change their livelihood or move their families and that's not an insignificant impact. See my thoughts and the article on [non-transferable communities ](https://scottvandenwarsen.com/reading/2026-04-12---Community-Disruption). 

I want to dig into the topic more and maybe talk to some folks who are involved here in Detroit. Waymo and driverless cars could be a technology of the near future, but what does that mean for the people depending on the technology of the past and present?
